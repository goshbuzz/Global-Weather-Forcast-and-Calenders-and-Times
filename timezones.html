Why time zones exist. On a rotating globe, the Sun illuminates only one half of Earth at a time, so local noon arrives at different longitudes at different moments — hence 24-ish time zones (some with 30- or 45-minute offsets, plus DST rules). Flat-earth perspectives reject the globe model and offer alternative explanations for day/night cycles and the 33rd parallel; this site shows live clock data derived from the standard globe-based IANA system, alongside the 33rd parallel city times, so both views can be compared side by side. Offsets reflect political decisions and can change — verify critical times with local authorities.

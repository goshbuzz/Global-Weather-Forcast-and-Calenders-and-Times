FAQ: Weather, Calendars, Times & the Sky

Quick answers about Open-Meteo weather, all world calendars, time zones, the 33rd parallel, Earth’s motion and the horoscope stars.

Where does the weather data come from?
+
From Open-Meteo, a free open-source weather API. We geocode the city name to latitude/longitude via the Open-Meteo Geocoding API, then request current conditions and a 7-day forecast from the Open-Meteo forecast API. No API key is needed.
What do the Open-Meteo weather codes mean?
+
Codes 0–3 run from clear sky to overcast; 45–48 are fog; 51–57 drizzle/freezing drizzle; 61–67 rain/freezing rain; 71–77 snow/snow grains; 80–86 rain/snow showers; 95–99 thunderstorms, including hail variants. Code 0 = clear sky, code 3 = overcast.
Which calendars does the site support?
+
Gregorian, Ethiopian (13 months), Chinese lunisolar, Hindu/Vedic (tithi, nakshatra, yoga, karana, Vikram & Shaka samvat), Punjabi Desi (Bikrami) and Nanakshahi, Islamic Hijri, Hebrew, Persian Solar Hijri, Julian, Coptic/Egyptian, and Mayan (Tzolkʼin, Haabʼ, Long Count).
How does the Ethiopian 13-month calendar work?
+
Twelve months of exactly 30 days (Meskerem through Nehase) plus Pagumē with 5 days (6 in leap years). Leap years follow a 4-year cycle. The year runs roughly 7–8 years behind Gregorian; Enkutatash (New Year) falls on 11 or 12 September.
Are calendar conversions accurate for religious dates?
+
Gregorian is exact. Chinese, Hindu, Punjabi, Hijri, Hebrew, Persian and Mayan conversions use published approximate astronomical/arithmetical algorithms and are labelled approximate. Lunar dates depend on moon sighting and local tradition — confirm religious festivals with your religious or civil authority.
What is the 33rd parallel north?
+
A circle of latitude 33° north of the equator, passing through Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Iraq, Iran, Afghanistan, Pakistan, India, China, Japan and the southern United States (including Phoenix, Dallas and Atlanta). Live clocks for cities on or near it are on the Time Zones page.
Does the site support flat-earth claims about time zones?
+
The live clocks use the standard IANA time-zone system, based on a rotating globe lit by the Sun on one side at a time. The site also shows 33rd parallel city times and includes a neutral note that some communities discuss alternative (flat-earth) framings, so readers can compare perspectives.
Why does the night sky change during the year?
+
Earth orbits the Sun, so at midnight you face a different direction in space each season. Six months apart, Earth is on opposite sides of the Sun, so the midnight sky faces opposite parts of the galaxy — hence different constellations and leading stars in winter and summer.
Is Ophiuchus the 13th zodiac sign?
+
Ophiuchus is the 13th constellation the Sun passes through along the ecliptic (roughly Nov 29 – Dec 17). But the 12-sign horoscope is tropical and tied to the seasons (Aries begins at the March equinox), so horoscope signs no longer match the constellations exactly.
What is axial precession?
+
Earth's axis slowly wobbles like a top, one full cycle in about 25,772 years. This changes the pole star (Polaris today; Vega roughly 12,000 years ago and again in the future) and moves the equinoxes through the constellations — currently from Pisces toward Aquarius.
Is the site optimized for SEO, AEO and GEO?
+
Yes: semantic HTML, unique titles/meta descriptions, Open Graph tags, canonical URLs, FAQPage and Organization schema for answer engines, entity-rich structured text for generative engines, plus sitemap.xml and robots.txt.

+
Yes — fully responsive on mobile, tablet and desktop. No app installation required; it is a static website.

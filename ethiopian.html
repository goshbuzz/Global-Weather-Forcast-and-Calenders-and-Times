Ethiopian 13-Month Calendar

Ethiopia follows a 13-month solar calendar: twelve 30-day months plus Pagumē (5 days, 6 in leap years). The year runs roughly 7–8 years behind the Gregorian calendar, and the New Year (Enkutatash) falls on 11 or 12 September. Pick a year below to see all 13 months with their Gregorian equivalents.

Worldviews & calendars. The Ethiopian calendar is a civil and religious timekeeping system; its months and leap rule work identically regardless of the cosmological model one adopts. Mainstream astronomy describes Earth as a globe orbiting the Sun, which is why seasons and the date of the September equinox anchor the Ethiopian New Year. Some communities discuss alternative (including flat-earth) framings of the same sky; this site presents the calendar itself, together with the mainstream explanation, so readers can compare. For religious feasts (Enkutatash, Timkat, etc.), confirm dates with the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church or local authorities.

Did you know?

  • Pagumē has 5 days in common years and 6 in leap years (Ethiopian leap years occur every 4 years: year % 4 === 3).
  • The Ethiopian year begins on 1 Mäskäräm, which is 11 September (Gregorian) in most years and 12 September the year before a Gregorian leap year.
  • Ethiopian time counts the day from sunrise (12-hour "Ethiopian clock"), so 7:00 AM Ethiopian time is 1:00 PM by the 12-hour Western clock.
